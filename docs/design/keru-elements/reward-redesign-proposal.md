# Elemental reward redesign: working proposal

The current non-ice rewards are mechanically repetitive placeholders. These designs replace them; numbers are initial tuning proposals, not simulation results. Each power must expose its counters, marked cards, and legal targets on the board, support touch and keyboard, and survive the 100-series carry/checkpoint rules. Rarity should add decisions or interactions, not just faster periodic triggers.

## Fire — press the advantage
- **Incite** (formerly Ember Cadence): consecutive receipts build Heat (provisional cap 4); at 2+ Heat a receipt deals +1 damage, and Heat expires at turn end. Candidate rarity progression: Rare cap 5; Epic permits spending 3 Heat for 2 damage; Legendary retains 1 Heat between turns. Card burns also feed existing heat-related effects as described below. These numerical details remain proposals.
- **Heart of the Phoenix**: when Hero is downed, deal fire damage to all enemies, stun them for one turn, and restore HP to Hero. Rarity affects fire damage, HP restored, and recharge. Common breaks and is permanently exhausted after it triggers; higher rarities may recharge after a number of encounters, a streak of consecutive successful moves, a total number of non-consecutive successful moves, or a combination. Exact numbers and recharge conditions are open tuning choices. Its state must visibly distinguish ready, recharging, and permanently broken; recharge progress and exhaustion must carry between 100-series encounters and restore on replay from the encounter checkpoint. Resolve the revival before declaring the party defeated, and specify the timing against simultaneous attacks and enemy defeat.
- **Scorch** is the candidate targeted card-burning ability described below. Hearthguard is removed; it should not return under another fire name.

## Nature — cultivate, connect, and protect
- **Living Bridge** (working name; rebrands Cinder Road): bridge a gap between tableau card ranks using a Nature-themed effect. The old fire Heat cost is removed. Exact activation, costs, rank gap, and rarity progression remain to be designed; the objective is a deliberate tableau-routing choice rather than another generic periodic rank match.
- **Regrowth**: gain 1 HP for every *n* successful moves. Rarity can increase HP rewards exponentially with consecutive sequencing streaks. The threshold, streak break conditions, growth curve, and any ceiling need tuning; display progress and potential healing so the benefit and balance are legible. Healing cannot exceed maximum HP unless another effect explicitly permits it.
- **BLOOM** (reworks Rootbridge): add an empty allied foundation and plant a seed with 10 HP. The seed is an allied foundation but cannot play golf. It gains one stack of **BLOOMING** each turn; after *n* turns, determined by rarity, it blooms into a Wildcard. When a Wildcard is played to a target, use the existing horizontal split double-value card system to display its interpretation after the play. Define target legality, chosen values, when a Blooming stack is gained, and what happens if the seed is defeated before bloom as implementation rules; do not silently assume those details are already settled. Display seed HP, Blooming progress, and the resulting Wildcard clearly. Persist seed and progress appropriately across encounters and replay checkpoints.
- **Thorns**: deal *n* damage to attackers for *n* turns. Damage, duration, eligible attacks, triggering frequency, and rarity scaling remain to be tuned and displayed. Replace the former generic Bloomguard proposal with this distinct retaliatory effect.
- **Vinebind** (working name): target a tableau column, lock it, and convert its affected cards to Nature. During automatic movement all other columns shift down, while the locked column does not shift. The lock also holds its cards to Nature, with a possible exception for a type Nature is weak to; the exception is not yet confirmed. Show the lock and affected cards. Duration, ordinary play access, and how replacements interact with the lock remain to be defined.

## Earth — endure, shape, and find resonance
- **Fossilize** (candidate replacement for Stone Shell): for the next *n* turns, damage that actually removes HP fossilizes into armor, at a rarity- and balance-dependent fraction of HP lost. The triggering hit first resolves normally; its new armor protects against later hits, not the same damage retroactively. Provisional rarity direction: Common gains 1 armor per 3 HP lost for 2 enemy turns; Rare 1 per 2 for 2 turns; Epic 1 per 2 for 3 turns; Legendary 1 per 1 for 2 turns. Values require testing, a clear armor cap and a decision on how fractional HP loss accumulates. This rewards surviving an initial impact and preparing for subsequent ones, instead of simply banking unused AP like Ice Armor. Specify what happens on a lethal hit and with existing armor before implementing.
- **RESONANCE** (reworks Seismic Pulse): drag the ability onto a tableau card. Starting with that card, recursively reach each horizontally, vertically, or diagonally adjacent card whose value is one rank above or below an already reached neighbor. Each reached card counts once, including the source; A and K wrap as in normal adjacency. Damage scales with the count. A card cannot be counted again when reached by multiple paths; empty positions stop that path. The user's example is a single-card resonance when the corner 4 has no adjacent 3 or 5, while the indicated 2 can cascade through A, K, Q and through 3 to another 2. Show a live preview of the complete connected cluster, count and expected damage before dropping. Recommend conservative linear damage first, with rarity changing AP cost, damage per card, or a capped secondary benefit; avoid multiplying the whole cluster exponentially until tested. Decide target enemy, whether cards are consumed (default proposal: no), split-value interpretation, and any propagation cap before implementation. The selection is a graph traversal over occupied tableau positions, not a straight-line chain.
- **BEDROCK**: apply to a selected tableau row, lock it, and convert all affected cards to Rock-type element. A locked row cannot be automatically shifted off the tableau, and the lock holds its cards to Rock, with a possible exception for a type Rock is weak to; the exception is not yet confirmed. Show both the locked row and converted cards. Duration, how other rows move around it, and whether conversion remains after the lock ends need a precise implementation rule. The existing element system calls its equivalent *Earth*, not Rock; resolve whether Rock is a display name for Earth or a distinct element before implementing, so visual and gameplay rules agree.

## Water — redirect and recover
- Flowing Current: once per turn, return the most recently received card to an empty reserve slot, restoring the preceding foundation rank; the hit and AP earned are not undone. The reserved card may be played normally, once. Rare reserve also accepts a card from an exposed column; Epic playing the reserve grants 1 AP; Legendary once per battle refresh the use.
- Tidal Step: once per turn exchange the exposed cards of two columns. Rare grants 1 armor after the exchange; Epic may exchange with the reserve; Legendary refreshes on an enemy stagger.
- Rainward: ending a turn with a card in reserve grants 2 armor. Rare 3; Epic retain the reserve across the next encounter; Legendary playing the reserved card heals 2 HP.

## Air — reach and movement
- Tailwind: once per turn reveal the card immediately beneath an exposed card; play it for 1 AP if legal. Rare first use costs 0; Epic restore the displaced exposed card on its column; Legendary reveal two deep.
- Windstep: once per turn move an exposed card to another column without playing it. Rare may move twice; Epic moving creates 1 AP if it reveals a legal play; Legendary may move an enemy-targeted card without triggering its attack.
- Gale Strike: the first receipt each turn from a different column than the previous receipt deals +1 damage. Rare first two switches; Epic +1 stagger on a switch; Legendary every switch, capped at 3 bonuses per turn.

## Shock — charge and discharge
- Dynamo: each receipt charges a visible meter to 4; at 4 choose whether to discharge for 2 AP or hold. Rare threshold 3; Epic excess AP becomes armor; Legendary discharge also adds 1 charge to another Shock power.
- Static Burst: spend 4 charge on a receipt to deal 3 bonus damage. Rare 4 damage; Epic also stuns a staggered target for its next action; Legendary excess damage arcs for 2 to a second enemy. No auto-discharge.
- Stormward: spend 3 charge for a ward that absorbs the next 3 damage. Rare absorbs 4; Epic unused ward returns 1 charge at turn end; Legendary a broken ward damages the attacker for 2.

## Light — protect and coordinate
- Dawnlight: the first time each turn a different ally receives a card, heal the most injured ally 1 HP. Rare heal 2; Epic cleanse one debuff on that ally; Legendary surplus healing becomes a one-hit ward of up to 2.
- Radiant Pulse: after two different allies receive cards in a turn, the next hit deals +2 damage. Rare threshold also accepts a familiar cast; Epic the hit adds 1 stagger; Legendary the next ally receipt also earns 1 AP.
- Guiding Light: once per turn select another living ally; their next receipt may use Hero's foundation rank for adjacency without changing Hero's rank. Rare grants 1 armor to that ally; Epic may select a familiar; Legendary the selected ally may use the bridge twice.

## Dark — sacrifice and exploit
- Siphon: after dealing damage to a debuffed enemy, heal 1 HP, once per turn. Rare twice per turn; Epic heal 2 against a staggered foe; Legendary surplus healing becomes 1 AP.
- Shadowstep: once per turn pay 2 HP (never lethal) to play a card two ranks away. Rare costs 1 HP; Epic that hit gains 1 stagger; Legendary refund the HP if the hit defeats its target.
- Night Strike: the first hit each turn against an enemy below half HP deals +2 damage. Rare +3; Epic also applies 1 stagger; Legendary a kill refunds 1 AP.

## Chaos — visible uncertainty
- Chaos Spark: once per turn choose a target and reveal a roll: either +3 damage or gain 1 AP. Rare damage becomes 4; Epic see the outcome before choosing the target; Legendary reroll once per encounter. Use seeded randomness and expose the outcome.
- Wildpath: once per turn reveal two random ranks; choose one as an additional legal rank for the next receipt. Rare add a third rank; Epic chosen rank persists until used or turn end; Legendary reroll the offered ranks once per encounter.
- Chaos Ward: once per turn choose to roll for either 1 or 4 armor. Rare minimum 2; Epic see the result before committing; Legendary may reroll once per encounter.

## Order — deliberate patterns
- Steady Guard: choose ascending or descending at turn start; completing three receipts in that direction grants 3 armor. Rare 4 armor; Epic one reversal is forgiven; Legendary a completed pattern retains 1 progress for the next.
- Measured Flow: complete a chosen three-receipt pattern to gain 2 AP. Rare 3 AP; Epic may choose an alternating pattern; Legendary excess AP converts to armor.
- Ordered Path: on completing a chosen pattern, the next exposed card matching the foundation rank is legal. Rare it may also be two ranks away; Epic keep the permission until used; Legendary completing the resulting play starts the next pattern with one progress.

### Initial tableau lock behavior

A row lock prevents that row from being automatically shifted off the field and locks its affected cards to the applied element. A column lock holds that column stationary while the other columns shift down. It likewise locks the affected cards to its element. A conversion to an element that the locked element is weak to may be allowed as an exception; this is a possibility for testing, not an established rule. Current opposed element pairs are not by themselves a definition of weakness. Show which cards and positions are protected and preview the shift. Confirm how a protected row interacts with the rest of a shifting tableau, how cards enter vacated positions, how overlapping row and column locks resolve, and how lock duration and card play work. Tests should cover both locks at the handoff, changed elements, expiring locks, and protected cards remaining in the correct positions.

## Implementation constraints
The proposed reserve, marks, anchors, charge meters, pattern choices, and seeded outcomes need dedicated state and presentation; they cannot be represented safely by the existing generic receipt helpers. Implement and test each mechanism before replacing its reward in the roll pool. In particular, ensure undone receipts cannot duplicate AP or hits; seeds and row/column locks resolve consistently across automatic movement; selectors work with keyboard and touch; and all state resets or carries according to the encounter checkpoint. Balance test each theme against the same encounter seeds and report results before treating the numbers as balanced.

## Tableau denial concepts (Eric's direction)

Fire can destroy a chosen tableau card to interrupt an enemy's possible line, at the cost of removing a resource the party might otherwise use. An initial ability candidate, **Scorch**, targets a visible card and spends AP; removing it is not a receipt and grants no chip, AP, or Momentum. For an exposed card, reuse the ordinary column refill behavior. For a buried visible card, remove that card by identity and refill behind the remaining cards without changing their order. Rarity could reduce cost, allow a buried target, add a small reward for disrupting a currently legal enemy card, or create an adjacent ember; none should silently destroy more cards. The precise target depth and numbers need playtesting.

Ice can **freeze a chosen card** against enemy plays for a limited number of enemy turns while leaving it available to the player. Treat the frost as attached to the card's ID, not its column or screen position. A frozen exposed card remains on top and blocks enemy access to cards underneath it; the enemy may choose another legal column. If the player takes it, the freeze ends with the card. A recommended simple initial rule is that ordinary automatic row movement still discards the frozen card when that card's row is due to leave; the marker is removed with it and never transfers to the replacement. This makes the timing and expiry legible and avoids silently delaying the whole board. An upgraded power could explicitly anchor it through one movement, but that is a separate effect requiring a defined refill/row policy. Freeze duration should count completed enemy turns, with expiry after the final protected enemy turn, and its remaining duration and impending row removal should be visible.

In the current 100-series implementation, enemy foundation selection scans exposed cards in column order (`dragonFoundationPlay`); automatic movement uses `withdrawToRow` to remove exposed cards by ID and replenish from behind at the enemy-to-party handoff. The implementation must exclude frozen IDs at every enemy card-selection path, clean up marks on every removal/redeal, and preserve the same behavior in both paced and instant enemy turns. Tests should cover a frozen first candidate with a later legal candidate, a blocked column, player collection, expiry, and a frozen card removed by the automatic row movement. Scorch tests should cover revealed next cards, replenishment, legal enemy sequence changes, and no receipt rewards. Both effects need accessible targeting, visible previews, and consistent solver/highlight behavior.

### Burning cards feeds existing heat effects

Every tableau card destroyed by a card-burning effect adds **one stack per card** to each applicable, already-active Heat-related buff and debuff on the battlefield, for players and enemies alike. Apply this to every card actually removed: a multi-card burn feeds the effects once for each removed card. A card that was not removed does not contribute. The burn removes the card as well as feeding the effects; it does not turn that removal into a tableau receipt.

For example, an enemy already at 2 stacks of Burning rises to 3 when one card is burned, and the card is still lost. If the player already has Heat Shield, it also gains one stack from that same card. The increase applies to existing effects; burning a card does not automatically create Burning, Heat Shield, or other heat effects on units that do not already have them. Respect any explicitly defined stack caps and make stack changes visible. Individual abilities can specify exceptions, but the default interaction is shared across all card-burning abilities, including multi-card burns such as Focus Fire.

Resolve each successful burn as a single action: determine and remove the affected cards, then apply one stack per removed card to the eligible active effects. Document separately whether each effect's duration refreshes; adding a stack alone should not silently refresh it. The current game has two distinct statuses displayed as “Burning” (`burn` and `cir-burning`), with different existing behavior; their stack semantics and Heat Shield's definition need to be reconciled explicitly during implementation rather than assuming they are interchangeable. Verify simultaneous effects on both teams, multi-card burns, caps, expiry, and no changes when no cards are removed.
